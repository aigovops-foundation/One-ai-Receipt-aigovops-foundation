# Review — SAIL, and what it can do for Beacon through One Receipt

29 September 2026 · for Ken and Bob · sources at the end. The link that prompted this review (pillar.security/sail-v1-2025) is the **v1** page; Pillar published **SAIL v2** on 8 July 2026 and it supersedes v1, so this review is against v2.

## What SAIL is

SAIL — the Secure AI Lifecycle — is Pillar Security's risk catalogue and roadmap method. Seven lifecycle phases (Plan · Discovery · Build/Posture · Test/Red-team · Deploy/Runtime controls · Operate/Sandbox · Govern), **91 numbered risks** (`SAIL x.y`), each with an example, affected assets, mitigations and a standards citation to ISO/IEC 42001 controls, NIST AI RMF, OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications (2026) and for LLM Applications (2025), EU AI Act articles, DASF and **AIUC-1 control ids**. v2 is agent-first: identities and delegation, tools and MCP servers, memory and RAG, per-action authorisation, three deployment zones (assets in code, cloud agents, endpoint agents) and autonomy tiers that set control intensity per agent. It ships as a Claude/agent skill (`pillar-labs/sail-skill`) that turns the catalogue into a roadmap, a maturity assessment, a compliance checklist or vendor questions.

Two facts shape how we can use it:

- **Licence.** CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 with an internal-use carve-out; redistribution or incorporation into products or services offered to third parties needs a separate licence. Our repos are Apache-2.0 / CC BY 4.0, so we **reference SAIL by id and never copy the text**. That is what `spec/crosswalks/sail-v2.md` does.
- **Authorship.** SAIL is vendor-authored and Pillar sells the platform that operationalises it. That is fine for a vocabulary; it is not a governance body, and our independence rule (no party grades its own interest) applies to it as to Glacis and AIUC.

## What SAIL is not — and where One Receipt fits

SAIL tells a security team *what could go wrong and what control should exist*. It says nothing about **evidence a stranger can verify**: no signing, no transparency log or proofs, no witnesses, no independent assessor, no receipt format, and nothing at all for the person on the receiving end — notice, explanation, challenge, review with authority, remedy are outside its scope. Its Govern phase asks for "detailed and consistent interaction logging", "tamper-evident storage" and "automated evidence capture at alert time" without saying what the evidence looks like or who can check it.

That gap is exactly One Receipt's shape. Read together: SAIL is the control catalogue; Umbrella declares which SAIL-mitigating controls apply; Beacon proves they ran on each event; Lantern reads it; One Receipt is the envelope that makes the proof portable and independently verifiable. Nothing needs to be invented on either side — SAIL becomes a `controls[].vocabulary`, and the receipt becomes SAIL's missing evidence layer.

## The improvements, project by project

**One Receipt (this repo — done in this PR).** `sail` and `owasp-agentic` added to the control vocabularies in the schema, TypeScript contract and spec; a crosswalk of the SAIL risks a receipt can evidence, field by field (`spec/crosswalks/sail-v2.md`); a SAIL row in the delta matrix; a thirteenth test vector — the Knight Capital case as a held agent action carrying `SAIL 5.13` (per-action authorisation) and `SAIL 6.14` (irreversible action with a named `reversal` remedy). Proposed for v0.3: a `transaction_type: lifecycle-event` so agent decommissioning (SAIL 7.7) is a first-class receipt rather than an extension.

**Beacon.** SAIL phase 2 (Discovery) is Beacon's strength and SAIL gives it a vocabulary buyers already recognise: Beacon's inventory row is the evidence for 2.1, 2.2, 2.6; its per-interaction receipt is the evidence for 7.1. Concretely: add SAIL ids to Beacon's checklist packs next to the NIST/EU/ISO ones; tag the `gate.evaluated` receipt's controls with `vocabulary: sail`; emit the model/version/BOM refs SAIL 2.x asks for in `identity.model`. And SAIL 5.7 / 7.4 (insecure telemetry, exfiltration via telemetry) is the security-team argument for the change the v0.2 adapter already requires — Beacon's receipts must stop carrying the OIDC subject, e-mail and plain prompt hashes.

**Umbrella.** Add SAIL as a target framework in the policy YAML alongside NIST AI RMF and the EU AI Act, so a UCID can cite the SAIL risk it mitigates and a policy pack can be generated "in SAIL's language" the way Pillar's skill generates checklists. SAIL 1.12 / 3.16 / 5.13 — an action-authorisation policy defined at build and enforced per action at runtime — is the Umbrella → Beacon → receipt chain in one sentence; say so in Umbrella's README.

**Lantern.** A "SAIL view" of a receipt graph: for each SAIL risk in the crosswalk, whether this transaction carries evidence for it (green), carries an omission reason (amber), or is silent (grey). That is the picture a CISO who lives in SAIL will ask for, and it is a filter over fields Lantern already reads.

**aigovops / Jeeves.** SAIL's agent-specific risks are Jeeves's: 2.7 (inter-agent topology), 5.18 (A2A impersonation), 5.20 (confused deputy), 7.7 (decommissioning). Each maps to receipts Jeeves already owns in the adoption plan — delegation receipts per hand-off, the requesting principal bound on every action, a closing receipt when an agent is retired. Name the SAIL ids in Jeeves's docs and the argument for the graph writes itself.

**prompt-studio.** SAIL 3.2 / 5.2 (insecure or tampered agent instructions) is the case for the `policy_commitment` per prompt version: a receipt whose commitment does not match the version on record is a tamper signal a verifier can see.

**practice (the café).** Two SAIL risks are corpus cases already: Knight Capital is 6.14 (irreversible actions without rollback) and Air Canada is 5.10 (policy-violating output). Add the SAIL id to those case cards and to the level-300 worksheet (hold and register), so a practitioner who arrives from a SAIL roadmap lands on the right door. SAIL 6.13 (approval fatigue) is a good Thursday topic: how many holds did a human clear this week, and did the SLA hold?

**vendor-RFI.** Pillar's skill generates vendor-assessment questions from the catalogue; ours should ask the follow-up SAIL cannot: *show the receipts*. For each SAIL runtime risk the vendor claims to mitigate, ask for a sample of `policy-decision` receipts at OR-2 or better carrying that SAIL id.

**The coalition.** Invite Pillar as a standards/protocol-community liaison (playbook group 3): review the crosswalk, agree the id citation, and ask for a licence carve-out so the crosswalk may quote risk titles. SAIL's AIUC-1 mapping is also a bridge into the UiPath/AIUC table we already want at the door.

## What not to do

Do not copy the SAIL catalogue into any Foundation repo (licence). Do not adopt SAIL's phases as our lifecycle — ours has **Contest**, theirs does not, and that is the point. Do not describe a receipt as "SAIL-compliant" or a system as "SAIL-certified"; a receipt carrying `SAIL 5.13` is a signed claim that a control ran, and the verifier's limitations say so.
