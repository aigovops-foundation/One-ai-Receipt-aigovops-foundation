# Global One Receipt Collaborative — invitation, commitments and funding playbook

How to invite every essential partner category, what each is asked to contribute, what authority each receives, and the appropriate funding request. Two failures this playbook exists to prevent: **vendor capture** and **unfunded participation**.

The offer is simple: help build an open, portable, privacy-protective evidence layer for consequential AI transactions. The return is early implementation influence, interoperable tooling, shared test artefacts, practical procurement alignment, and a governance structure in which no operator, log provider, assessor or insurer certifies its own commercial interest. Not a proprietary badge; not a special outcome.

## Three bodies

Technical Incubator (open core; rough consensus; no member veto; money supports work, never buys outcomes) · Public Interest Council (binding on conflicts, privacy, accessibility, contestability, equity, data sovereignty, high-risk profiles; paid; protected budget line) · Adoption and Pilot Network (pilots, procurement language, usability and outcomes; recommends and publishes; never certifies its own pilots). The Foundation is the initial neutral convener and maintainer, not the long-term sole registry steward, assessor qualifier, notary operator or assurance issuer — every invitation says so.

## Five commitments in every invitation

1. **Open core** — schemas, verification rules, vectors and reference implementations public under royalty-free, implementation-friendly terms.
2. **No pay-to-control** — funds buy capacity, not votes, certifications, endorsements, standards status or special technical treatment.
3. **Independence** — no organisation assesses, certifies or operates the sole log for a system it operates or materially controls at higher assurance levels.
4. **Public-interest power** — civil society, affected-user, accessibility, privacy, Indigenous and Global South participants receive compensated participation and real voting and appeal rights.
5. **Running code and evidence** — claims advance only with vectors, independent implementations, pilot results and published limitations.

## The invitation package

One-page letter · two-page charter and conflict-of-interest policy · v0.2 architecture summary (transaction graph, versioned predicates, privacy profile, witnessing, contestability, assurance levels) · a 90-day workplan · a contribution menu (cash, in-kind, pilot, research, translation, governance) · the funding and independence policy · the LOI template · the line "Participation does not imply endorsement of any vendor, product, certification, insurer or legal interpretation." The first ask is a **45-minute listening session, not money**.

## Founding cohort

Not a logo wall. A balanced first cohort of **18–24 parties**: 3–4 AI/agent/application providers (no provider holds more than one technical co-chair seat); 2–3 cloud/logging/identity/security providers; 3 standards or open-source liaisons; 3 buyers/procurers from different sectors and regions; 2 independent audit/forensic/legal-evidence partners; 2 insurer/broker/reinsurer participants; 4–6 paid civil-society, accessibility, affected-community, Global South and Indigenous partners; 2–3 independent research/evaluation partners; regulators as observers where lawful. The announcement emphasises balance, independence rules, open artefacts and paid public-interest governance — never sponsor size.

## Outreach order

1. Adjacent standards and open-source communities — reconcile, do not duplicate.
2. Independent civil-society, accessibility, Global South and Indigenous partners — legitimacy before vendors arrive.
3. Two or three implementers willing to ship pilots and two independent infrastructure/log witnesses.
4. Buyers and procurers who can define evidence rights.
5. Independent researchers to pre-register evaluation.
6. Auditors, insurers and regulators as reviewers of evidence and adoption paths.
7. Additional sponsors only after the balance above exists.

## Funding request sequence

**Step 1 — listening invitation:** a meeting, a named contact, permission to share the v0.2 package; no tier. **Step 2 — role-fit memo (one page):** the exact problem this party can help solve, the workstream and time, the conflict rules, the contribution routes, the request or offer. **Step 3 — non-binding LOI** (`LOI-TEMPLATE.md`). **Step 4 — workplan and agreement:** only after scope, governance rights, data handling and outputs are agreed; separate agreements for technical contribution, sponsorship, research grant, community participation grant and pilot data access.

## Templates

**General invitation** — *Subject: Invitation to shape an open, verifiable AI transaction-evidence standard.* We are convening the Global One Receipt Collaborative: an open effort to make a bounded AI transaction independently verifiable without exposing prompts, weights or other protected content. Buyers, users, regulators, auditors and providers often cannot later establish what an AI system was authorised to do, which policy evaluated the action, which model, runtime and tools were involved, whether a record changed, or how a person can challenge the result. Existing standards solve pieces; One Receipt is an interoperable evidence layer that connects them. We are inviting a deliberately balanced first cohort. Participation confers no certification, endorsement or control; the core is open, royalty-free, independently testable, and governed so no operator, log provider, assessor or insurer grades its own interest. We would value a 45-minute listening conversation; the initial ask is feedback on scope, risks and fit — not a commitment.

**Commercial capacity-sponsor follow-up** — *Subject: Proposed One Receipt contribution: implementation and independent evaluation.* Following our discussion, we propose that [Organisation] participate as a [Implementer / Infrastructure / Adoption / Risk Evidence] Partner for an initial twelve months. Requested contribution: cash [range], publicly disclosed as a capacity contribution; in kind [named commitment]; participation [delegate roles and hours]. In return: open-process participation, shared test artefacts, inclusion in the factual interoperability matrix after successful tests, and a role in the relevant workstream. This conveys no special vote, certification, exclusive integration, procurement preference, or ability to approve claims about [Organisation]'s own systems. The contribution agreement includes conflict disclosures, funding transparency and publication independence.

**Public-interest participation invitation** — *Subject: Paid governance invitation: One Receipt Public Interest Council.* We are forming a paid Public Interest Council for an open protocol that records and verifies limited evidence about consequential AI transactions. We seek organisations that can exercise real decision power over notice, explanation, challenge, human review, remedy, accessibility, privacy, community authority and the risk of audit-washing. This is not an endorsement request and not unpaid consultation: the role carries voting and appeal authority, a participation grant of [amount], accessibility and language support, and the right to publish dissent. We would first like to hear whether the proposed structure is credible, what safeguards are missing, and whether participation would be useful on your terms.

**Research invitation** — *Subject: Independent evaluation partnership — security, privacy, usability and contestability of AI receipts.* We seek independent research partners to test whether signed, privacy-minimised AI transaction records actually improve verification, user understanding, challenge access and evidence quality — studies that can find failure, not validation by default: privacy and linkability attacks, log-equivocation tests, accessibility and language studies, challenge and remedy outcomes, adoption burden for small and Global South implementers. Funding is a research grant with methodological and publication independence.

## Ready to launch when

A published charter, contribution policy, IP policy, conflict register, code of conduct and decision process exist; paid public-interest and regional participants are in the first cohort before the announcement; no commercial contributor above 15%; two independent implementations and two independent log/witness paths committed to the first interop event; one buyer with a real procurement or audit-evidence use case; one research partner with a pre-registered evaluation; the public delta matrix published; and launch language with no unsupported claim about compliance, safety, fairness, admissibility, premium reduction or certification.

**Bottom line.** Ask vendors for money and implementation work; ask buyers for real requirements and pilots; ask standards communities for convergence; ask researchers to test failure; ask civil society and affected communities to exercise paid power; ask regulators to stress-test without endorsing.
